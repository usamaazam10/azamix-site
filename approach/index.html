How we work

No black boxes. No surprises.

Stages
Discuss · Define · Build · Deliver
Estimates
Based on an agreed scope

Every project runs on the same four stages: discuss, define, build, deliver. Small projects move through them quickly; larger ones repeat the build stage several times. Either way, you always know what's happening, what we need from you and what comes next.

{% include section-head.html label="The stages" id="process-title" title="Four stages, always in the open." %}
  1. 01

    Discuss

    We learn how the work happens today, who will use the software, what it must connect to and what a good outcome looks like. Real examples, such as the spreadsheets, forms or tools involved, tell us more than descriptions.

    From you: a description of the problem, and time with the people who do the work.

    A shared understanding of the problem and open questions

  2. 02

    Define

    We separate what the first version must do from what can wait, choose the technical approach, and design the key screens and data model where the project needs them. Uncertain parts, such as a third-party integration or an AI feature, are identified and tested early.

    From you: decisions on priorities and trade-offs.

    A written scope, technical plan and estimate

  3. 03

    Build

    We build in short stages and share working software regularly on a test environment or test build. Your feedback shapes what comes next, and any change to scope is discussed with its effect on time and cost.

    From you: try each build and tell us what's wrong or missing.

    Working software at regular intervals

  4. 04

    Deliver

    We test the release, deploy it to production, and hand over the source code, documentation and access. If you want ongoing support, such as updates, monitoring and improvements, we agree it separately.

    From you: acceptance testing, and accounts in your organisation's name for hosting, stores and services.

    A live system you can run, and support if you want it

{% include section-head.html label="Quality" id="quality-title" title="Built for year three, not just launch day." %}

Security and privacy

Permissions are checked on the server, secrets are kept out of the code, and only the data the software needs is collected. These decisions are made during planning, not after launch.

Testing

Important business logic has automated tests, and each release is checked by hand on the devices and scenarios that matter to your users.

Maintainable code

Established, well-supported technology and a clear structure, so another competent developer could take the software over.

Documentation and handover

Setup and deployment instructions, architecture notes and the reasons behind major decisions, written during the project rather than reconstructed at the end.

{% include section-head.html label="Estimates and changes" id="estimates-title" title="Honest numbers, agreed up front." %}

Estimates follow the scope

A price or deadline given before the requirements are understood is a guess. We estimate once the scope is agreed, and state the assumptions behind it. A smaller first release is often the quickest way to learn what matters.

Changes are decisions

Needs change once people use working software, and that's normal. When they do, we explain the effect on time and cost so you can decide, rather than letting scope grow quietly.

{% include section-head.html label="Confidentiality" id="confidentiality-title" title="What you share with us stays with us." %}

We don't publish client names, screenshots or project details without permission. If you'd like a non-disclosure agreement before sharing details, mention it in your first message and we'll review it before you send anything sensitive. During a project we ask only for the access and data the work needs.
