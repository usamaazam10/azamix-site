{% include section-head.html no="01" label="Problems it addresses" id="problems-title" title="Work that's repetitive, but not quite simple." lede="Some tasks are fully predictable and need no AI at all. Others involve reading, sorting or writing, which is where language models can help. Most real workflows contain both." %}

  • Re-typing data from documents

    Invoices, order forms, applications and delivery notes arrive as PDFs or emails, and someone copies the details into another system. Extracting the fields automatically, with a person checking the uncertain ones, can remove most of the typing.

  • Sorting and routing requests

    Support emails, enquiries and internal requests need to reach the right person. Classification can route the clear-cut cases immediately and flag the rest.

  • Copying data between systems

    Exports, imports and copy-paste between tools that don't talk to each other. This usually needs no AI at all, just a reliable integration and a schedule.

  • Writing the same kind of reply

    Responses that follow a pattern but need adjusting each time. A drafted reply that a person edits and sends can save time without handing over judgement.

  • Finding information in internal documents

    Policies, manuals and past records exist, but finding the right paragraph takes too long. Search that understands questions, and cites its sources, can help.

  • Not knowing what's realistic

    It's hard to tell which AI claims apply to your work. Testing a specific use case on your own examples answers that question with evidence.

{% include section-head.html no="04" label="Technical considerations" id="tech-title" title="What responsible AI integration involves." lede="Calling an AI API takes minutes. Making the result dependable takes design. These are the parts we plan for on every AI feature." %}

Evaluation

Before building the full feature, we collect real examples with known correct answers and measure the model against them. The same test set is rerun whenever the instructions or model change, so improvements and regressions are visible.

Human review and error handling

Language models can produce confident, wrong answers. Outputs are validated against expected formats and rules, uncertain results go to a review queue, and failures are retried or flagged rather than silently dropped.

Data privacy

We decide exactly which data needs to be sent to an AI provider, mask or remove personal details where possible, and check the provider's data-use and retention terms with you before real data is processed.

Operating costs

AI APIs charge by usage. We estimate costs from your real volumes, choose the smallest model that meets the accuracy you need, avoid sending unnecessary text, and monitor spend over time.

Limitations

Models don't know your business unless the relevant information is supplied with each request, their outputs vary between runs, and providers update them. A good design assumes all three.

Provider independence

We keep the AI step behind a small interface in your system, so changing provider or model later is a contained change rather than a rewrite.

{% include section-head.html no="06" label="Ownership" id="ownership-title" title="Models change. Your workflow keeps working." %}

Models change

AI providers release new models and retire old ones on their own schedule. Keeping the test set and rerunning it before switching models is part of maintaining an AI feature. Ongoing support can be agreed separately.

Accounts and ownership

We recommend AI provider accounts and API keys belong to your organisation, so usage, billing and data agreements are in your name. Ownership of the code and deliverables is set out in the project agreement.
